## 1. Executive Summary

CHC is the portfolio's only "At Risk" Business Unit, and the reason is resourcing, not demand. It has the fastest-growing, highest-momentum market of the four BUs (IQVIA-confirmed) but the portfolio's weakest field-force health, coverage, and call frequency — and consequently its weakest sales achievement. This review asks the Executive Committee to decide how, not whether, to close that resourcing gap, and separately whether CHC's small current size should be weighed against or alongside its disproportionately high return on incremental investment.

## 3. Executive Verdict

**FACT:** CHC scores highest of all four BUs on Growth Potential and Market Competitiveness, and lowest on Commercial Performance and Customer Engagement.
**INTERPRETATION (Confidence 78):** the gap is resourcing — CHC's field-force vacancy (26.5%) is more than double every other BU's.
**What this is not:** a market problem, a targeting problem, or (on current evidence) a proposition problem.

The chain's key implication: the target is very likely not the problem — the market evidence argues it was reasonable. Structured Decision Graph rendering of this chain (cause → effect → recommendation → impact → KPI) is a tracked future enhancement, not yet built — see `PLATFORM_ROADMAP.md`.

## 10. Strategic Decision Points
*Presented as scenarios for the Committee to weigh — not resolved automatically. Per framework §3a, ranking CHC #1 on Expected ROI does not by itself mean CHC should receive the next investment dollar.*

### Strategic Investment Scenario: CHC vs. portfolio resourcing priority

CHC ranks **#1 of 4 on Expected ROI** (18.7 — roughly 6x the next-best BU) and **#4 of 4 on Strategic Importance-by-size and Market Opportunity** (it is the smallest BU by revenue and market value today). Both are true simultaneously; neither cancels the other.

| 10 | Risk profile | Operational risk 60.2 (moderate); competitive/regulatory risk not modeled | Partially data-backed |
| 11 | Portfolio strategy | Is CHC core, adjacent, or non-core to corporate strategy? | **Executive input required** |
| 12 | Corporate priorities | Is CHC a board-level priority independent of current size? | **Executive input required** |

**Options on the table, none pre-selected:**

- **A. Prioritize CHC now** on the strength of dimensions 2–4, 6–9 — accept the size/strategic-importance trade-off because expected marginal return is highest.
- **B. Hold CHC at current resourcing**, prioritize a larger/more strategically-weighted BU instead — accept leaving the highest-ROI opportunity partially uncaptured.
- **C. Partial/staged investment in CHC** while gathering the missing qualitative inputs (dimensions 1, 5, 11, 12) before committing fully.

This review does not select between A/B/C — that requires dimensions 11 and 12, which only the Executive Committee can supply. Section 15 (Executive Outlook) reflects Option C as the pack's working assumption only because it is the lowest-regret path while those inputs are gathered — not a modeled recommendation.

## 16. Business Review Appendix
*Analyst-facing. Methodology, assumptions, confidence, data quality.*

**Methodology:** `BUSINESS_REVIEW_FRAMEWORK.md` — 8-dimension Health Index, 6-dimension cross-BU ranking, 12-dimension Investment Consideration Matrix, all computed by `js/business-review-engine.js` (BU-agnostic, validated against all four current BUs).

**Basis differences carried through this review:** (a) Sales = YTD calendar Jan–May 2026; IQVIA = trailing MAT window — different time bases, directionally comparable only; (b) Sales' internal invoiced value and IQVIA's IMS-modeled LCV are different currency bases despite both being EGP — comparisons here use each source's own internal ratios, never raw cross-source totals.

**Confidence limitations:** 5 months of internal Sales history only, no prior-year Sales comparison; Coverage snapshot is a single month (June); SFE headcount is a point-in-time snapshot, not trended; brick-level granularity not yet available.

**Quality Gate — the standing test (framework §12):** *"Would this help the CEO and Executive Committee make a better decision than a traditional BI dashboard?"*

**Honest answer: yes, conditionally — not yet unequivocally across every future BU.** For CHC specifically: yes — a BI dashboard would show four disconnected KPI panels and let the reader guess at causation; this pack traces vacancy → coverage → frequency → sales with named confidence levels, and surfaces the CHC size-vs-ROI tension as a decision rather than auto-resolving it, which no standard BI tool does. What keeps the answer conditional rather than unequivocal, named rather than hidden: (1) the narrative layer above the Health Index/ranking is still analyst-assembled per BU, not yet auto-generated — full automation is the platform's AI Executive Analyst phase, not yet built; (2) three of the twelve Investment Consideration dimensions (profitability, portfolio strategy, corporate priorities) have no data source yet and must be manually supplied every time; (3) brick-level granularity, needed to resolve the "concentrated vs. even vacancy" hypothesis, doesn't exist yet. None of these invalidate the CHC read above — they define exactly what has to be true before this scales to Cluster/DIAB/GIT with the same confidence, and before it can be called unequivocal rather than conditional.
